A lot of frontend candidates treat their resume and portfolio as the same pitch, repeated twice. They're actually solving different problems.

## What a resume is for

A recruiter or ATS scans it in seconds, mostly looking for keyword matches (React, TypeScript, years of experience) and basic pattern-matching against the job description. Its job is narrow: **don't get filtered out.**

## What a portfolio is for

Once you're past the screen, whoever reviews your portfolio has more time and a different question: not "do you have the right keywords" but **"can this person make good decisions."** This is where architecture choices, tradeoffs you explain, and real projects (not tutorial clones) actually matter.

## The mistake

Cramming project explanations into your resume (bullet after bullet of "built X using Y") wastes the resume's narrow window on detail it can't actually communicate well. And a portfolio that's just a reworded resume wastes the deeper attention it's been given.

## The takeaway

Keep your resume sharp, keyword-matched, and skimmable. Save the *why* — your reasoning, your tradeoffs, what you'd do differently — for the portfolio, where someone is actually willing to read it.
